# Seedance 2.5 — cuts and transitions

Cut setting = `[transition type guide] + [basic constraint requirements] + [cut logic requirements]`

Two valid approaches:

- **Broad** — let the model choose: *"Please choose the most suitable one according to the style of
  this film from transition methods such as [natural shot switching / mask transition / ink
  transition / similar object transition]."*
- **Specific** — name the transition and describe the exact A→B mechanics for that cut point.

**The constraint clause goes on every transition**, verbatim:

> (prohibit rigid cutting, prohibit objects from appearing out of thin air)

Without it, Seedance hard-cuts and pops objects into frame.

---

## Foundation transitions

### Natural shot switching
Effectively a cut, but with breathing room — A becomes B directly, no effect, yet the camera must
feel like it breathes and the durations must be coordinated.

> Use "natural lens switching" transition at the cutting point (prohibit rigid cutting, prohibit
> objects from appearing out of thin air). Maintain the coordination between the breathing sensation
> and duration of the lens, leave enough switching time, let picture A transition naturally to
> picture B, and ensure the coherence of the narrative.

*Use for:* the vast majority of conventional narrative. Clean, doesn't drag. Tests the shot content itself.

### Fade in / fade out
A darkens to black (or white), then B emerges and brightens.

> Use "fade in and fade out" transition at the cutting point (prohibit rigid cutting, prohibit
> objects appearing out of thin air). Picture A gradually darkens until completely black (or
> completely white), then picture B slowly emerges from the darkness and gradually brightens.

*Use for:* openings and endings, passage of time, the complete end of a plot beat.

### Stacking (dissolve)
A becomes transparent as B emerges; the two overlap briefly.

> Use "stacking" transition at the cutting point (prohibit rigid cutting, prohibit objects appearing
> out of thin air). As picture A slowly becomes transparent, picture B gradually appears, producing
> a soft overlapping and blending effect of 1-2 seconds between the two pictures.

*Use for:* memory, dreams, slow passage of time, lyrical or emotionally soft transitions.

### White flash / black flash
An instant of pure white or black, then the next shot.

> Use "white flash/black flash" transition at the cutting point (prohibit rigid cutting, prohibit
> objects appearing out of thin air). The screen instantly bursts into a strong white light (or
> instantly plunges into complete darkness) and flashes by, followed by a rapid transition to the
> next scene, with a strong visual impact.

*Use for:* impact, hits on a beat (drum, gunshot, shutter), beat-sync edits, tension.

### Erase (wipe)
B pushes A off frame like a sliding door.

> Use "erase" transition at the cutting point (prohibit rigid cutting, prohibit objects appearing
> out of thin air). Picture B smoothly advances from the left (or right/up/down) like a sliding
> door, completely covering/erasing picture A.

*Use for:* retro/old-fashioned styling (Star Wars), or an obvious change of location.

## Creative transitions

### Mask transition (whip-through-object)
Camera pushes into an object that fills frame to black; the next shot pulls out of the black,
already elsewhere.

> Use "mask transition" at the cutting point (prohibit rigid cutting, prohibit objects appearing out
> of thin air). The camera pushes forward and is completely blocked by an object in the picture —
> such as a big tree or a person's back — causing the full screen to turn black. Then the camera
> passes through the darkness and directly pulls out to reveal the next brand new scene.

*Use for:* spatial crossings — through a mirror into another world; a sheet of paper covers the
lens and reveals a new scene.

### Similar object transition (match cut)
Last frame of A and first frame of B share shape, outline, or color.

> Use "similar object transition" at the cutting point (prohibit rigid cutting, prohibit objects
> appearing out of thin air). Utilize the similarity of shape or color for seamless transition. For
> example, in the last frame of the previous shot there is a circular moon in the sky; as the shot
> advances, the moon seamlessly transforms into the circular latte art in the cup in the next shot.

*Use for:* montage. Full moon → coffee crema. Copper coin → the end of a ruler.

### Action transition (whip pan)
Camera swings hard in A, motion-blurs, and B cuts in from the blur on the same vector.

> Use "motion transition" at the cutting point (prohibit rigid cutting, prohibit objects appearing
> out of thin air). Utilize the rapid motion of the lens for connection. In scene A, the lens
> vigorously swings to the left (or right), creating a strong motion blur; then in scene B, following
> the same swinging direction and force, cut in from the blur and settle the frame.

Two flavors: **similar movements** (softer) and **conflicting movements** (more forceful).

*Use for:* action, parkour, travel vlogs — a sense of instant transfer.

### Dynamic relay transition
The character's own movement carries the cut: they exit frame up in A, fall in from the top in B.

> Use "Dynamic Relay Transition" at the cutting point (prohibit rigid cutting, prohibit objects
> appearing out of thin air). Use the continuous movements of the character to switch scenes: in
> scene A the character jumps up and leaves the frame, then in scene B the character maintains a
> falling posture and falls from the top of the frame into the picture.

*Use for:* outfit-change videos ("kick your shoes to change clothes", "snap to change clothes"),
protagonist travelling between regions.

### Zoom in / zoom out transition
A detail of A is magnified to the extreme until it becomes the whole of B — or the reverse.

> Use "zoom-in/zoom-out transition" at the cutting point (prohibit rigid cutting, prohibit objects
> appearing out of thin air). The camera zooms in on a detail in scene A (such as the pupil of a
> character) to the extreme. When the pupil occupies the entire screen, the image seamlessly unfolds
> inside the pupil, becoming a grand panorama of scene B.

*Use for:* grand-narrative or micro-perspective shifts; sci-fi; "a world within a world" nesting.

### Ink painting transition
A blooms away like ink dropped in water; B emerges from the spreading ink.

> Use "ink and wash transition" at the cutting point (prohibit rigid cutting, prohibit objects
> appearing out of thin air). Picture A quickly blends and spreads like thick ink dripping into clear
> water. As the ink spreads, picture A gradually disappears, and picture B gradually emerges from the
> scattered ink.

*Use for:* Eastern aesthetics and poetry — hanfu showcases, water-town scenery, historical
documentary, long memory sequences.

## Notes

- On a **cut long video**, pick the transition per cut point and write it into that time slice.
- On a **seamless transition between two uploaded videos**, use the dedicated template in
  `task-templates.md` §9 — it additionally requires "do not modify @Video 1 and @Video 2."
- Locking a single take is the opposite instruction and equally explicit: state one continuous shot,
  no cuts.
